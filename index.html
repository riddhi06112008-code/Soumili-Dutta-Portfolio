MOMO-MESH AI

An AI concept and forecasting dashboard for Wow Momo, built to cut waste in quick-service restaurant delivery.

Strategy & Analytics

The problem

Wow Momo's revenue crossed ₹600 Cr in FY25, but losses widened to ₹148 Cr. It spent ₹1.27 to earn every ₹1 of operating revenue.

Most QSR AI work focuses on chatbots, loyalty offers and customer support. The real leak is inventory waste, delivery margin loss and outlets whose demand does not match the forecast.

Chicken filling, wrappers and sauces go to waste in one outlet while the next one loses sales.

The idea: move inventory between outlets, not just forecast it

Layer 1. Hyperlocal demand prediction

Uses weather, Google events, college festivals, IPL matches, Swiggy and Zomato promotions and historical sales to predict each outlet's demand every hour.

Layer 2. Dynamic inventory marketplace

Think of it as Uber for momo inventory. Every outlet is both a supplier and a consumer, and the system flags surplus, shortages and transfer opportunities before waste happens.

Third Wave Coffee: break-even analysis

Business Mathematics project at TAPMI (Section B, WAS Group 7). We used linear functions to find how many Classic Cold Coffees the café must sell each month to cover its costs.

Business Mathematics
My contribution: applying linear equations and generating graphical insights using data.

The model

Selling price per cup₹275
Variable cost per cup₹99.85
Monthly fixed cost (assumed)₹1,50,000
Contribution per cup₹175.15
RevenueR(x) = 275x
Total costC(x) = 150000 + 99.85x
ProfitP(x) = 175.15x − 150000

Setting R(x) = C(x) gives x = 150000 ÷ 175.15, which rounds up to 857 cups a month, about 29 cups a day. That is ₹2,35,675 in revenue.

Price is from the published menu. Variable cost applies the 36.31% FY2024 material-cost ratio to the price. Fixed cost is an illustrative assumption, so this is a model and not outlet data.

Raj Shamani: Brand the Persona

A rhetorical analysis of a podcaster and business communicator, followed by a rebranding concept for how he could be positioned next.

Rhetoric & Rebranding
My role: research, applying rhetoric and SWOT analysis.

Understanding the rhetoric

Writer
Entrepreneur, podcaster, business communicator
Audience
Gen Z, millennials, professionals, aspiring founders
Context
Digital learning, career uncertainty, ambition
Purpose
Inform, inspire, build audience trust
Medium
Podcasts, interviews, social media
Strategy
Curiosity, credibility, simple explanations

Ethos

Borrowed credibility. Expert guests transfer authority to Raj, and repeated association builds trust. Risk: guest authority can overshadow the host.

Pathos

Authentic storytelling. Guests share struggle, failure and lessons, so success feels achievable instead of distant.

Logos

Knowledge translator. He turns expert experience into problem, insight, framework and action that audiences can apply.

SOAP: repositioning the persona

NowFuture
SpeakerInterview hostFuture-ready mentor
OccasionKnowledge sharingCapability building
AudienceLearnersFuture-ready young leaders
PurposeExpert accessPersonal transformation

SWOT: the brand audit

SRelatable communication, influential guests
WMany roles blur the core identity
OOwn a future-readiness mentorship space
TAudiences remember guests, not the host. More podcast competition.

The gap and the rebrand

Today the brand says "Raj interviews successful people". The gap is that insight does not always become action. The concept, Raj Shamani 2.0, positions him as India's trusted future-readiness mentor, with pillars in AI, careers, finance, leadership and enterprise.

After
